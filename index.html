Our approach

Past ecosystems, present questions.

The Biodiversity and Paleogenomics Group studies the evolutionary and ecological processes shaping population and species distributions across space and time.

We work with ancient DNA recovered from bones, sediments, museum and archaeological material, and environmental archives. By combining molecular evidence with ecological, geological, and archaeological data, we investigate both natural and human-driven environmental change—from recent history to the Holocene and Pleistocene.

Research themes

Methods that connect deep time and living systems.

Ancient DNA & paleogenomics

DNA from bones, sediments, museum collections, and other archives to reconstruct past biodiversity, population history, and ecological change.

Biodiversity through time

Evolutionary and ecological drivers of species distributions, including climate change, landscape transformation, and human impacts.

Environmental reconstruction

Interdisciplinary work combining genomics with archaeology, zooarchaeology, geology, palaeoecology, and reproducible computation.

Research environments

From cave deposits to environmental DNA.

We use multi-proxy approaches to explore long-term biodiversity and ecosystem dynamics. Our research integrates sedimentary DNA and ancient DNA with contextual information from field, museum, archaeological, and geological collections.

Our work is collaborative by design: meaningful reconstructions of past environments require evidence and expertise across disciplines.
